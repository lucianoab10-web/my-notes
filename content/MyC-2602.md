### 1. From _Ciencias de la Tierra_ (Tarbuck & Lutgens, 8th ed.)

This textbook perfectly mirrors the structure of your class presentation. You should focus your reading on **Capítulo 3: Materia y minerales**.

  

Specifically, go directly to the section titled **"Propiedades físicas de los minerales"** (starting on page 88). This section covers the exact diagnostic properties listed in your slides:

  

- **Forma cristalina** (Crystal shape / habit)
    
      
    
- **Brillo** (Luster - metallic vs. non-metallic)
    
      
    
- **Color y Raya** (Color and Streak - using a porcelain plate)
    
      
    
- **Dureza** (Hardness and the Mohs scale)
    
      
    
- **Exfoliación y fractura** (Cleavage and fracture)
    
      
    
- **Peso específico** (Specific gravity / density)
    
      
    
- **Propiedades secundarias:** The book also covers the secondary properties mentioned at the end of your presentation, such as magnetism, taste (_sabor_), feel (_tacto_), and smell (_olor_).
    
      
    

Before reading the physical properties, it is highly recommended to read the preceding section in Chapter 3, **"Estructura de los minerales"** (page 86). It explains how the internal geometric arrangement of atoms dictates the external physical properties like cleavage and crystal shape.

  

### 2. From _Química: La Ciencia Central_ (Brown)

While Tarbuck explains _what_ the physical properties are, your chemistry textbook explains _why_ they exist at an atomic level. General chemistry textbooks do not cover field identification (you won't find chapters on streak or Mohs hardness), so you should use it strictly to understand the underlying atomic mechanics:

  

- **Tipos de Enlace (Chemical Bonding):** Study the differences between ionic, covalent, and metallic bonds. For example, understanding that metallic bonds allow electrons to move freely explains why certain minerals have a _Brillo Metálico_ (Metallic luster) and conduct _Electricidad_ (Electricity).
    
      
    
- **Fuerzas Intermoleculares (Intermolecular Forces):** Study weak bonds like Van der Waals forces. This is the exact reason why _Exfoliación_ (Cleavage) occurs. A mineral breaks along the planes where the atomic bonds are the weakest.
    
      
    
- **Estructuras Cristalinas (Crystal Structures):** Review the geometry of solids, specifically the concept of density and unit cells, which directly controls a mineral's _Peso específico_ (Specific Gravity) and its outward _Forma y hábito_ (Habit).
