### 3. Secondary Literature: Commentaries & The Aconceptual/Postphenomenological "Measuring Rod"

These sources provide both the direct analysis of Naess’s Gestaltism and the theoretical framework needed to test it against Pylkkö:

#### A. Direct Commentaries on Naess’s Gestalt Ontology

1. **Diehm, Christian (2006).** _"Arne Naess and the Task of Gestalt Ontology"_, _Environmental Ethics_, 28(1), 21–35.
    
    - _Role:_ Focuses strictly on Gestalt ontology as a liberation of nature from Galilean reductionism rather than an extension of human self-interest.
        
2. **Speranza, Andrea (2006).** _Ecología profunda y autorrealización: introducción a la filosofía ecológica de Arne Naess_ (Biblos).
    
    - _Role:_ Excellent Spanish-language commentary explaining Naess's adoption of Gestalt psychology (Wertheimer, Koffka) and the critique of primary/secondary qualities.
        
3. **Fox, Warwick (1995).** _Toward a Transpersonal Ecology_ (SUNY Press, Chapters 4 & 5).
    
    - _Role:_ Analyzes Naess’s "relational field" and how Gestalt perception functions as a non-dual ontology.
        
4. **Tatray, Andrew (2006).** _Rebuilding the Foundations of Deep Ecology: A Nondualist Approach_.
    
    - _Role:_ Examines direct intuition, non-dual perception, and overcoming Cartesian dualisms in Naess.
        

#### B. The Pylkkö / Postphenomenological Measuring Rod

5. **Pylkkö, Pauli (1998).** _The Aconceptual Mind: An Essay on Film, Consciousness and Philosophy_ (John Benjamins).
    
    - _Role:_ Primary source for Pylkkö's radical aconceptualism, postphenomenology, asubjectivity, and his rejection of formal symbolic/relational modeling.
